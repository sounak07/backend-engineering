## 3. Recurring themes

1. **Spreadsheet/formula-engine questions are Harvey's single most repeated interview theme by a wide margin.** At least 7 distinct thread titles in the 1Point3Acres question bank are variations on "design a spreadsheet with formula cells, dependency graphs, and cycle detection" — evaluated via DFS/topological sort, caching, and O(1)-read constraints layered on incrementally. This is almost certainly because Harvey's actual product includes spreadsheet-like structured legal data with computed fields. **This is the single highest-leverage thing to practice before interviewing here** — build a working formula-cell evaluator with cycle detection from scratch, cold, more than once.
2. **Citation/text-matching questions are the second cluster** — inserting citation tags, matching document spans to sources, word-level tagging. This maps directly to Harvey's actual "cite your sources" legal-AI feature. Practice string/interval-matching problems framed around "find where in this text a given source appears" rather than only abstract LeetCode string problems.
3. **In-memory file systems and versioned KV stores** form a third, smaller cluster — generic LLD classics (Trie-based filesystem, `mkdir`/`ls`/`addContentToFile`), but still likely chosen because they mirror Harvey's document-storage/versioning ("Vault") product surface.
4. **Escalating, multi-part live problems, evaluated on running code** — same house style you saw at Stripe and Harness. One candidate confirms syntax lookup is allowed (not a closed-book memory test), but a red-black tree rebalancing question required actual running code, not just verbal approach.
5. **System design is explicitly reported as the weakest-defined round** — at least one candidate flagged unclear expectations around depth/pacing as the one process complaint in an otherwise well-regarded loop. Worth proactively asking your interviewer early in that round how much depth vs. breadth they want, rather than guessing.
6. **AI-product-specific rounds exist** (LLM engineering: prompt design/evaluation, hallucination-catching) that don't appear at any of the other six companies in this set — worth a distinct prep bucket if your loop includes one, separate from generic system design.
7. **Small, tight-knit interview loop** — Glassdoor explicitly notes "their question bank seems to be small," and multiple 1Point3Acres threads recur around the same few problem families, so depth of practice on the spreadsheet/citation clusters specifically will likely pay off more than broad LeetCode grinding.

## 4. Process / round-structure notes

- Typical loop (per Glassdoor and multiple aggregators): **Recruiter screen (~30 min)** → **Technical phone screen (LeetCode-medium-equivalent, multi-part)** → **Onsite/virtual-onsite, 4 rounds**: 1 coding (LeetCode-style), 1 system design, 1 behavioral, 1 project deep-dive. Senior+ roles add a **founder interview**.
- One recent candidate reported a cleaner, more structured version: 30-min technical + 30-min hiring-manager + a final 2-hour session broken into 4 separate interviews, with the recruiter clearly explaining the purpose of each round in advance — worth asking your own recruiter for this breakdown if it isn't offered.
- Glassdoor aggregate: **36.6% positive interview-experience rating**, difficulty **3.02/5**, average time-to-hire **~20 days** — the lowest positive-experience rating and fastest hiring timeline of the seven companies in this set; consistent with a fast-moving, still-small startup rather than a large, process-heavy org.
- Difficulty is explicitly reported as **"failing the vast majority of engineers"** for Senior SWE roles specifically — treat this as a genuinely selective bar despite Harvey's small size and non-household-name status.
- Interviewers are reported as **nice, helpful, and fair** even when the bar is high — multiple accounts describe positive interpersonal experiences (help with hints, willingness to swap a weak-area question) even amid rejection.
